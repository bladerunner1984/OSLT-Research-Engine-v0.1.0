# Sponsor capability self-assessment — Serverity Ltd

**Status:** working draft for founder review. Not submitted. Not legal advice.
**Purpose:** establish honestly whether a small research company can discharge sponsor
responsibilities for a non-CTIMP study, and name what is missing.

---

## Why this document exists

The assumption that sponsorship requires a university or NHS body is **wrong**, and it
cost this project weeks of treating a soluble problem as a wall.

The NIHR sponsorship toolkit defines a sponsor as *"the individual, organisation or
partnership that takes on overall responsibility"*, and states that the host organisation
acting as sponsor **is not a requirement**. The UK Policy Framework does not require a UK
legal representative for studies other than CTIMPs.

The test is **capability**, not affiliation. Organisations are under no obligation to
sponsor, and the assessment is case by case — so the question is whether Serverity Ltd can
evidence the responsibilities below, not whether it is the usual kind of body.

---

## The study this concerns

Prospective cohort, ~1,900 participants, questionnaire and interview data. **No
intervention, no investigational medicinal product, no clinical procedure.** Non-CTIMP,
low physical risk. Risk profile is informational and psychological, not clinical — which
matters, because sponsor obligations are explicitly proportionate to risk.

---

## Assessment against UK Policy Framework s9.10

| Sponsor duty | Position | Evidence or gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientifically sound, addresses systematic review of existing evidence** | **Strong** | Pre-registered specification, frozen and hash-chained before outcome analysis. A 6,428-record corpus covering all 64 propositions with counterevidence lanes searched. Feasibility census and power analysis complete. |
| **Independent expert review has occurred** | **GAP** | None. The specification has had no methodological review. This is the single largest gap and cannot be closed from inside the company. |
| **Suitability of investigators and research teams** | **Weak** | Sole researcher. No second coder. Sponsor cannot meaningfully assure the suitability of a team of one that includes itself. |
| **REC and other approvals obtained before start** | Neutral | Not yet sought; this assessment precedes it. |
| **Adequate insurance or indemnity** | **GAP — costed** | Negligent-harm indemnity required. Non-negligent harm insurance determined by REC on risk. Professional indemnity and public liability for a low-risk non-interventional study is an ordinary commercial product, not a research-grade barrier. **Needs a quote before any claim of capability.** |
| **Adequate finance** | **Unresolved** | ~1,900 participants. No funding identified. Recruitment, participant time and data collection costs are real and unquantified. |
| **Competent risk and data management** | **Strong** | Data boundaries enforced in code: TRE-class records carrying person-level payload raise on construction. Provenance admission, dependency collapse, claim ceilings, hash-chained computation journal. Publicly auditable, 442 tests, continuous integration. |
| **Monitoring and reporting procedures** | **Partial** | Automated gates and an append-only journal exist. Human oversight procedures — who reviews, when, against what — do not. |
| **Data accessible after completion with consent and privacy safeguards** | **Strong in design** | Release gate implements a nine-point standard; a model review cannot satisfy the human review requirement by type. Consent process not yet drafted. |

---

## Honest verdict

**Three of nine are strong, and unusually so.** Most sponsors assert good governance in
policy documents. This one can point at gates enforced in code, running under CI, that
have demonstrably refused output — including six retracted papers admitted as evidence and
three of the project's own results.

**Three are genuine gaps, and two of them cannot be closed alone:**

1. **Independent expert review.** Needs one external methodologist. Unavoidable.
2. **Suitability of the research team.** A team of one, self-assured by the sponsor, is a
   circularity a REC will see immediately.
3. **Finance for ~1,900 participants.** Unquantified.

**Insurance is the most tractable gap and should be quoted, not assumed.** It has been
treated as a wall throughout this project without anyone obtaining a price.

---

## Recommended sequence

1. **Ask the HRA directly** whether a small research company can sponsor a study of this
   design, and what evidence of processes and systems they expect. Draft email prepared.
2. **Obtain an indemnity quote** for a non-interventional questionnaire and interview
   study with ~1,900 participants. Converts an assumed blocker into a number.
3. **Secure one independent methodologist.** This closes the review gap *and* the second
   coder gap *and* strengthens the ONS application. One person resolves three problems.
4. **Scope a smaller first study.** Nothing requires the full 1,900 at once. A pilot of
   150–200 would test recruitment, consent, instruments and coder reliability at a
   fraction of the cost, and a successful pilot is the strongest possible argument to a
   later sponsor, funder or collaborator.

Point 4 is probably the most important line in this document. The programme has been
scoped as all-or-nothing, and it does not have to be.

---

## What this document does not claim

That sponsorship is assured. That the gaps are minor. That any of this substitutes for
advice from the HRA or an insurer.

It claims one thing: **the sponsor route was never formally closed, and it was abandoned
on an assumption rather than on an answer.**
