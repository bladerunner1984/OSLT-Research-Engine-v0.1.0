# OSLT Scientific Constitution and Competing Models v2.4 RC1

## Objective
OSLT is designed to determine which combination of intrinsic/recognition, developmental/neuropsychiatric, social/digital, educational, clinical, academic, institutional, policy, ascertainment and cultural mechanisms best explains specified outcomes and their historical change. It must be capable of rejecting the investigator's preferred explanation.

## Non-negotiable rules
1. A preferred hypothesis is metadata, not a conclusion.
2. The system must retrieve and analyse counterevidence and null evidence.
3. Identity labels, distress, referral, treatment request, treatment initiation and later pathway change are separate outcomes.
4. Association, prediction, causation, explanatory contribution and certainty are separate quantities.
5. No individual is assigned a cause from population-level association.
6. Neurodevelopmental or psychiatric diagnoses are not causal conclusions.
7. Cultural/religious variables are contextual hypotheses unless individual-level designs justify more.
8. Discourse/semantic change is not causal proof.
9. Academic paper counts/citations are not truth measures; dataset dependency is collapsed.
10. All causal specifications and decision rules are frozen before outcome analysis.

## Competing model families
- **Intrinsic/Recognition:** stable or intrinsic differences plus changes in recognition, acceptance and access.
- **Multifactorial Developmental-Institutional:** heterogeneous pathways involving developmental, psychiatric, social, digital, educational, clinical, academic, institutional, policy and cultural mechanisms.
- **Ascertainment/Service:** changes in thresholds, coding, disclosure, service capacity, measurement and administrative completeness.
- **Mixture/Heterogeneity:** different mechanisms dominate in different subgroups, cohorts, eras and jurisdictions.
- **Null/Alternative:** apparent effects may be common-cause, reverse-causation, measurement or selection artefacts.

The 64-proposition registry is the operational test catalogue. Each proposition has a prediction and a condition capable of weakening or falsifying it.
